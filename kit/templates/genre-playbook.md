# Template: genre playbook

Every file in `genres/` has these sections, in this order, with these headings. Follow the [writing standard](../../CONTRIBUTING.md): stopwatch, number, screen, and a source for every fact.

---

# <Genre name>

> **The feeling sold:** one sentence in a child's words. Example: "I own cool stuff and nobody can take it. I take theirs."

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Core verb | The one action repeated most |
| Study these first | 3 to 5 live games, each with one reason it is worth studying, each linked to its teardown |
| Session that works | Typical minutes per session in the leaders |
| Players per server | The values the leaders use, and why |
| Who owns the genre | Whether old leaders hold the players or new games can enter, with numbers |
| Build cost | Small team weeks for a first version, and what makes it expensive |
| Money comes from | The 2 or 3 product types that carry revenue |
| What kills clones | One sentence |

## Why people play it

The need it meets, who the players are (age skew and device if known), and what they would say they like about it. Keep it to what a source supports; mark the rest **Our read:**.

## The loop, with a stopwatch

A table for the leading game (name it), then a line on how the other leaders differ.

| Step | Player does | Seconds | Sees and hears | Number that moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Then the loop stack: what closes every few seconds, every minute, every 5 to 10 minutes, every session, every day, every week.

## Numbers that work

Real tables from the leaders (prices, odds, timers, curves), each with its source and date. Then one block headed **Start with:** giving starting values for a new game, derived from those tables, with the ratio or reasoning shown.

## What brings them back

Three short parts: later in the same session, tomorrow, next week and after. For each, the mechanism a leader uses, with its numbers.

## Other players

Server size and why. What a player sees of others. What they can do to or with each other. What can be lost and how it is protected. The moment that gets clipped.

## Money

What the leaders sell, with Robux prices (table). Where in the flow the offer appears. What does not sell or backfires in this genre. Policy points that apply (for example paid random items).

## Live ops

How often the leaders update, on what day, and what an update contains. What happens to player counts when updates stop. What a small team can sustain.

## How these games die

| Symptom you can measure | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|

At least five rows, each tied to a real example where one exists.

## Checklist

Ten to fifteen pass/fail checks with numbers. A reader should be able to hold their own game against this list in ten minutes.

## Twists and open space

Variations that have already produced hits (sourced), then angles that look under-served. Everything in the second half is **Our read:**.

## Sources

Links to the teardowns and the main external sources used on the page.
