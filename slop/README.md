# Slop

This section answers two questions:

1. How do you make slop that holds players?
2. What separates a slop game that reaches millions from the thousands of copies that reach nobody?

Read it in order the first time. After that, [04-build-it.md](04-build-it.md) is the page to work from.

## What "slop" means here

On Roblox in 2025 and 2026, players, press and developers use "slop" for games that are made fast, follow a trend, wear a meme skin, and monetise early. An academic study of the scene treats "slop" and "shovelware" as the same word and describes teams of 5 or 6 teenagers and young adults who buy and resell scripts and prefer flat pay to revenue share "because most games fail" ([International Journal of Communication, 2026](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/download/25738/5347/109408?inline=1)).

The word describes how a game is made. It says nothing about how many people play it. The three biggest concurrent-player counts in Roblox history all belong to games people call slop:

| Game | Built by | Peak concurrent players | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steal a Brainrot | One developer (SpyderSammy), on rights bought from an earlier game | 25.4 million, October 2025 | [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/robloxs-steal-a-brainrot-becomes-first-game-to-surpass-25m-concurrent-players/) |
| Grow a Garden | A 16-year-old, first version in about three days | 22.3 million, 23 August 2025 | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden) |
| Steal An Egg | Two developers, owned by DoBig Studios | 14.3 million, 19 September 2026 | [RoStats](https://rostats.io/records) |

So "quality slop" is a real category. It means a simple, small-server, collect-and-earn game with a meme skin that is tuned well and operated every week.

## The short answer

Everything below is explained, with its numbers and sources, in the pages that follow.

1. **The first thing you buy pays for itself in under 30 seconds.** In Steal a Brainrot it costs $25 and earns $1 a second.
2. **Your wealth stands in the world, not in a menu.** Anyone walking past your base can count it.
3. **Every purchase is also a lottery ticket.** About 1 in 6 comes out upgraded, about 1 in 100 comes out ten times better.
4. **Four to eight players per server, bases side by side.** Few enough to know, close enough to envy and to rob.
5. **What you own can be taken, and protecting it runs on a timer of about a minute.** Defence becomes a reason to stay.
6. **Shops restock on short clocks.** Five minutes for the common shop, 30 or 60 for the rare ones. Each restock has a small chance of something rare.
7. **It keeps growing while you are away.** Leaving costs nothing; coming back pays.
8. **One live hour a week.** A Saturday event with an item that only exists that day. It produces 5 to 35 times the weekday player count.
9. **Resetting changes what you can do.** Each rebirth adds a slot, a longer lock and a new tool, on top of a multiplier.
10. **There is nothing to read.** The title is the instruction. The description is five lines of verbs.

And the two things that decide whether a copy lives:

11. **It changes the verb.** Steal became break-and-steal, then throw, then kick. A same-verb reskin from one of the biggest studios on the platform has 7 players.
12. **It is updated every week, on the same day, for as long as it lives.** Every hit checked was updated within three days. Every dead clone had stopped.

## The honest part

- **Most of the players leave.** Steal a Brainrot sat at about 0.17% of its peak on a weekday morning in November 2026, and Grow a Garden at about 0.08%. A game can earn a great deal on the way down (Steal a Brainrot's purchases were estimated at US$64 million by March 2026, per [Bloomberg](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/03/25/robloxs-hit-game-steal-a-brainrot-battles-its-many-imitators)), but the curve is the curve. See [05-after-the-peak.md](05-after-the-peak.md).
- **Most attempts fail.** The same Bloomberg piece counts hundreds of brainrot competitors with more released daily. The people in the study above take flat fees for that reason.
- **The audience is children.** About 35% of Grow a Garden's players were under 13 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)). Several of the mechanisms here work by loss, envy and scarcity. Each page says where the line is, and [foundations/15-the-line.md](../foundations/15-the-line.md) explains why it is there.
- **The platform has moved against the weakest version of this.** Since June 2026 Roblox's recommendations look at 28 days of a player's behaviour, not 7, and since August 2026 a game that rewards players for watching a video feed is removed from the catalogues children see. A slop game that only has a first hour now has a ranking problem as well as a retention problem.
